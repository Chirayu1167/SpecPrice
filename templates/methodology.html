Methodology & Dataset

How the price estimate is produced, what the dataset covers, and what the model can and cannot tell you.

What SpecPrice predicts

SpecPrice estimates the typical price associated with a phone's technical specifications, based on patterns learned from the 2026 dataset.

What it does not predict

It is not a live retail-price tracker and does not directly account for temporary discounts, retailer offers, launch age, supply/demand, or every model-specific pricing factor.

Why estimates can differ from retail prices

Two phones with similar specifications can have very different prices because brand positioning, software support, design, processor generation, launch timing, and market strategy also affect pricing.

Training Pipeline

  1. Dataset collection: smartphone listings with hardware specifications and launch/market prices (2026 vintage, India market, INR).
  2. Feature engineering: numeric specs (RAM, storage, battery, camera, display), ratio features (charging watts per mAh), and one-hot encodings for brand, processor brand, OS, and charging class — 85 features in total.
  3. Target transform: the model regresses on log(price) to keep premium-segment errors proportionate; estimates are exponentiated back to INR (price = exp(model.predict(X))).
  4. Missing values: handled natively by XGBoost's missing-value branches. The app fills unknown inputs with representative defaults rather than fabricating values.
  5. Model: XGBoost regressor with 300 trees, max depth 5, trained with early stopping on a holdout split.
  6. Deployment: the trained model and its column schema are serialized into the two .pkl artifacts loaded by this web app. On startup the app verifies the 85-column schema matches the model's trained feature order exactly.

The Buy Advisor

The advisor compares the current market price you enter against the specification-based estimate:

BUY NOW Market price ≥2% below estimate — strong deal.
GOOD TIME Price within ±2–5% — fair value.
WAIT Price >5% above estimate — target a seasonal sale window, where 5–11% drops are typical.

Caveats

  • Sale-window estimates are a seasonal heuristic (Republic Day, festive week, Big Billion Days, Black Friday, year-end), not price forecasting.
  • Ultra-premium estimates (>₹1,00,000) are less reliable due to sparse dataset coverage.
  • Estimates reflect hardware value in the training market; brand positioning and launch hype are captured only indirectly through the brand encoding.
